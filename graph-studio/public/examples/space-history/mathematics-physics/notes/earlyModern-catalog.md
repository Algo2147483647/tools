# Calculation, experiment and analytical mechanics: event catalog

[Period guide](./earlyModern.md) · [Overview](../README.md) · [Names](../NAMES.md)

## 1543 CE · Mikołaj Kopernik publishes a heliocentric planetary system

ID: `copernicus` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

The mathematical reorganization of planetary motions placed Earth among the moving planets. Circular motions and inherited devices remained, so the book should not be equated with the later elliptical and dynamical account.

Date: Publication year, not the beginning of the author's investigations. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Copernicus/)

## 1545 CE · Girolamo Cardano publishes methods for cubic and quartic equations

ID: `cardano` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

The publication brought together results involving Scipione del Ferro, Niccolò Tartaglia and Ludovico Ferrari. Its complicated history illustrates collaborative transmission, contested credit and the limits of a lone-inventor narrative.

Date: Publication year; several methods were discovered earlier. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Cardan/)

## 1572 CE · Rafael Bombelli develops arithmetic with complex quantities

ID: `bombelli` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

His algebra supplied systematic rules for expressions involving square roots of negative numbers. Such quantities could help solve equations with real answers, before their later geometric and analytic interpretations.

Date: Publication of the first three books. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Bombelli/)

## 1591 CE · François Viète introduces a systematic symbolic algebra

ID: `viete` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

Letters represented known and unknown quantities in a general analytic art. The notation made relationships more general while differing from the symbols and conventions that later became standard.

Date: Publication landmark. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Viete/)

## 1600 CE · William Gilbert investigates magnetism

ID: `gilbert` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

Experiments with magnets and a spherical model supported the proposal that Earth behaves magnetically. The work distinguished many magnetic and electrical phenomena, although the later unified electromagnetic theory lay far ahead.

Date: Publication year of the major study of magnets. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Science History Institute: early magnetism](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/laws-of-attraction/)

## 1609 CE · Johannes Kepler publishes elliptical planetary motion

ID: `kepler-orbits` · Astronomy and cosmology · Mathematics and Physics

Analysis of observations led to elliptical orbits and the area law for Mars. Mathematical modeling was constrained by precise measurements; the physical explanation through universal gravitation came later.

Date: Publication of the first two planetary laws. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Kepler/)

## 1610 CE · Galileo Galilei publishes telescopic observations

ID: `galileo-telescope` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

Lunar terrain, many stars and satellites of Jupiter challenged inherited views of the heavens. These observations strengthened new cosmological arguments without by themselves proving every part of a heliocentric system.

Date: Publication year of the telescopic report. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Galileo/)

## 1614 CE · John Napier publishes logarithms

ID: `napier` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Logarithms converted difficult products and divisions into more manageable arithmetic for astronomical and other calculations. The original construction and scale differed from the later natural and common logarithm conventions.

Date: First publication. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Napier/)

## 1619 CE · Johannes Kepler relates orbital periods and distances

ID: `kepler-harmonic` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

The squared orbital period was related to the cube of the characteristic orbital distance. This quantitative regularity became an important target for later dynamical explanation.

Date: Publication year of the third planetary law. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Kepler/)

## 1621 CE · Willebrord Snell derives a refraction relation

ID: `snell` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

The sine relation for refraction supported mathematical prediction of light paths. Earlier work by ابن سهل contained a related relation; independent rediscovery and delayed publication complicate priority claims.

## 1631 CE · Thomas Harriot's algebra appears posthumously

ID: `harriot` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

The published work developed equation manipulation and symbolic techniques, including inequality signs. Editorial selection after the author's death matters when separating surviving publication from the broader manuscript work.

## 1632 CE · Galileo Galilei stages a debate over world systems

ID: `galileo-dialogue` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

A dialogue brought arguments over terrestrial and celestial motion to a broad readership. The subsequent proceedings show how institutional authority, patronage and interpretation shaped the reception of natural philosophy.

Date: Publication year; the trial followed in 1633. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1635 CE · Bonaventura Cavalieri publishes the method of indivisibles

ID: `cavalieri` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Areas and volumes were compared through families of lower-dimensional elements. The method proved powerful but raised questions about rigor that later limit-based and integration theories addressed differently.

Date: Publication landmark. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Cavalieri/)

## 1637 CE · René Descartes connects algebra and geometry

ID: `descartes` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

The Geometry related geometric constructions to equations and transformed the study of curves. Modern coordinate practice developed through several contributors and should not be projected unchanged into this text.

Date: Publication as an appendix to a methodological work. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Descartes/)

## 1637 CE · Pierre de Fermat records a claim about powers

ID: `fermat-margin` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

A famous marginal claim asserted that no positive integer solutions exist for powers greater than two. The surviving story of a lost proof does not establish that a valid general proof was known at this time.

Date: Traditional approximate date of the marginal note; published posthumously in 1670. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fermat/)

## 1638 CE · Galileo Galilei publishes mathematical studies of motion

ID: `galileo-motion` · Mechanics and gravitation · Physics

The work developed quantitative treatments of acceleration, projectiles and the strength of materials. Idealized mathematical arguments and experimental practices together helped reshape mechanics.

Date: Publication year; investigations extended over many earlier years. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Galileo/)

## 1644 CE · Evangelista Torricelli interprets the mercury barometer

ID: `torricelli` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

A mercury column and the space above it became evidence in debates about atmospheric pressure and vacuum. The instrument connected physical theory with reproducible changes in height and environment.

Date: Letter and interpretation landmark; experiments began earlier. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Torricelli/)

## 1654 CE · Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat analyze games of chance

ID: `pascal-fermat` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

Correspondence on the problem of points supplied systematic methods for dividing stakes in an interrupted game. This was an influential episode in probability, not the beginning of all reasoning about uncertainty.

Date: Dated correspondence. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Pascal/) · [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fermat/)

## 1657 CE · Christiaan Huygens publishes a treatise on chance

ID: `huygens-probability` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

The text organized calculations of expectation and gambling problems into a teachable mathematical subject. Later probability would extend well beyond the finite and symmetric situations emphasized here.

## 1662 CE · Robert Boyle reports a pressure-volume relationship

ID: `boyle` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

Air experiments related pressure and volume under controlled conditions. The empirical relationship applied approximately at fixed temperature; later thermodynamics and kinetic theory supplied broader frameworks.

Date: Publication of the experimental relation; the temperature qualification is a modern formulation of its domain. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Improved instruments made small-scale structures available to public scrutiny through images and descriptions. The work links optical technology, observation and the interpretation of previously inaccessible physical objects.

## 1665 CE · Isaac Newton develops early methods of fluxions

ID: `newton-fluxions` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Private investigations connected changing quantities, tangents and quadrature. The development took place over several years, and later publication must be distinguished from the date of manuscript work.

Date: Landmark for work during 1665–1666; not public dissemination of a finished calculus. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1672 CE · Isaac Newton reports experiments on light and color

ID: `newton-light` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

Prism experiments supported the claim that white light contains differently refrangible components. The argument reinterpreted color as a property of components of light rather than a simple modification by glass.

Date: Publication of the optical paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Newton/)

## 1676 CE · Ole Rømer infers a finite speed of light

ID: `roemer` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

Timing discrepancies in eclipses of a satellite of Jupiter were interpreted through changing light travel time. The argument connected astronomical observation with a physical property that was difficult to measure terrestrially.

Date: Announcement year; later numerical estimates refined the result. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor: early theories and measurements of light](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Light_1/)

ID: `newton-principia` · Mechanics and gravitation · Mathematics and Physics

Laws of motion and universal gravitation connected planetary trajectories, falling bodies and tides within a mathematical framework. The achievement relied on earlier observations and mathematics and did not immediately resolve every physical problem.

A wavefront construction explained reflection and refraction in an assumed medium. Later wave optics retained important geometrical ideas while transforming their physical interpretation.

## 1696 CE · Johann Bernoulli poses the fastest-descent problem

ID: `bernoulli-brachistochrone` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics and Physics

The challenge asked for the curve of least travel time under gravity. Competing solutions helped make optimization of entire curves a central problem, preparing the ground for the calculus of variations.

Date: Public challenge year; solutions followed in 1697. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

ID: `bernoulli-large` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

The treatise included a theorem relating observed frequencies to underlying chances under stated assumptions. This established a mathematical form of the law of large numbers, rather than a guarantee about every finite sample.

## 1736 CE · Leonhard Euler studies the bridges of Königsberg

The impossibility argument reduced a walking puzzle to connections and vertex degrees. This became a foundational example for graph theory, a subject broader than the original geographical problem.

## 1736 CE · Leonhard Euler develops analytical mechanics

ID: `euler-mechanics` · Mechanics and gravitation · Physics

Calculus-based methods recast mechanics in a systematic analytical language. This helped move beyond the geometric presentation of earlier dynamics and supported work on both particles and continuous bodies.

A mechanical model connected pressure with particle impacts. It was a significant precursor to kinetic theory, though the later statistical framework and experimental support developed over a much longer period.

The problem linked a physical system with a partial differential equation and traveling-wave solutions. Disagreement about allowable initial shapes exposed important questions about functions and mathematical representation.

A major introduction to analysis brought functions, infinite series and exponential and trigonometric relations into a systematic presentation. This enlarged the language used to model continuous change.

Correspondence with Leonhard Euler introduced a method for optimizing functionals without relying on particular geometric constructions. Its later role in mechanics illustrates a direct connection between mathematical technique and physical formulation.

The posthumous paper, communicated by Richard Price, addressed inference about an unknown chance from observations. Later Bayesian methods generalize the argument and make explicit modeling choices that are not supplied automatically by the theorem.

A systematic formulation used generalized coordinates and variational reasoning to handle constrained motion. The work made a shared mathematical structure visible across apparently different mechanical systems.
