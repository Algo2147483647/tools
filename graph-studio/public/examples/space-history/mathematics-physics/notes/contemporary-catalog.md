# Modern structures, experiments and computation: event catalog

[Period guide](./contemporary.md) · [Overview](../README.md) · [Names](../NAMES.md)

## 1948 CE · Renormalized quantum electrodynamics becomes quantitatively predictive

ID: `qed` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

Work by 朝永振一郎, Julian Schwinger, Richard Feynman and Freeman Dyson reorganized calculations of interactions between light and matter. Agreement with precise measurements made the theory powerful without implying that every mathematical foundation was thereby settled.

Date: Landmark for developments during 1947–1949, not a single simultaneous publication. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel research summary: quantum electrodynamics](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1965/summary/)

## 1948 CE · Claude Shannon establishes a mathematical theory of communication

ID: `shannon` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

Information and channel capacity became quantities that could be analyzed independently of a message's meaning. The entropy formula has structural connections with statistical physics, but the two uses require careful specification of the underlying model.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Shannon/)

## 1950 CE · Laurent Schwartz publishes the theory of distributions

ID: `distribution` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Generalized functions supplied a rigorous setting for derivatives of nonsmooth objects and idealized sources. They became fundamental tools for partial differential equations and the mathematical language of fields.

Date: First volume of the systematic treatise; the theory had been developed earlier. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Schwartz/)

## 1950 CE · John Nash proves existence of equilibria in finite games

ID: `game-theory` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

A fixed-point argument established an equilibrium under specified assumptions about mixed strategies. It joined topology with models of strategic behavior, while not asserting that real participants always find or prefer such an equilibrium.

Date: Short proof publication; a fuller paper followed in 1951. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Nash/)

## 1954 CE · 楊振寧 and Robert Mills formulate a non-Abelian gauge theory

ID: `yang-mills` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Mathematics and Physics

Local internal symmetry was extended beyond the electromagnetic case. The original physical application was incomplete, but the mathematical structure later became central to the description of fundamental interactions and to geometry.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1957/yang/facts/) · [Original non-Abelian gauge-theory paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.96.191)

## 1956 CE · 李政道 and 楊振寧 question parity conservation in weak interactions

ID: `parity-proposal` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

A critical review showed that assumed mirror symmetry had not been adequately tested in weak processes and proposed experiments. Theoretical questioning and experimental demonstration were distinct contributions.

Date: Proposal publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original paper questioning parity conservation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.104.254)

## 1957 CE · 吳健雄 and collaborators demonstrate parity violation

ID: `parity-test` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

Measurements of beta decay from oriented cobalt nuclei showed a directional asymmetry inconsistent with parity conservation. The experiment overturned an accepted symmetry assumption; its authorship is not represented by the associated prize recipients alone.

Date: Experimental results published in 1957, following work in late 1956. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original experimental parity-violation paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.105.1413)

## 1957 CE · John Bardeen, Leon Cooper and J. Robert Schrieffer explain conventional superconductivity

ID: `bcs` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

A many-electron quantum theory used paired states to account for key superconducting properties. Its scope does not automatically include every later family of unconventional superconductors.

Date: Theory publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original theory of superconductivity](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.108.1175)

## 1957 CE · Alexandre Grothendieck develops homological methods in algebraic geometry

ID: `grothendieck` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

A major paper organized derived functors and related structures in a broad categorical setting. The approach helped make cohomology a portable language for connecting geometric and algebraic problems.

Date: Publication of the foundational homological-algebra paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Grothendieck/)

## 1960 CE · Theodore Maiman operates a ruby laser

ID: `laser` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A working device produced coherent optical amplification through stimulated emission. It followed earlier theoretical and microwave developments and enabled new experiments in spectroscopy, precision measurement and light-matter interaction.

Date: First operation year; the patent and priority history is broader. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [AIP archival history of the first lasers](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/laser/)

## 1963 CE · Edward Lorenz demonstrates deterministic nonperiodic flow

ID: `lorenz` · Mechanics and gravitation · Mathematics and Physics

A simplified convection model displayed sensitive dependence and irregular trajectories. The result made limits of long-term prediction tangible without implying that deterministic equations are random or universally unpredictable.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1963 CE · Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer announce the index theorem

ID: `atiyah-singer` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

A theorem connected the analytical index of an elliptic operator with topological data. It became a major meeting point of analysis and geometry and later informed quantum-field-theoretic reasoning.

Date: Announcement year; detailed proofs and extensions followed. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1964 CE · Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig propose constituent classifications

ID: `quarks` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

Independent quark and ace models organized hadrons through a smaller set of constituents. Evidence for the physical parton structure and the later theory of strong interactions must be distinguished from the initial classification proposals.

Date: Independent proposal year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [CERN historical account of the quark proposals](https://cds.cern.ch/record/1998511)

## 1964 CE · Several groups develop a gauge-symmetry-breaking mechanism

ID: `symmetry-breaking` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

François Englert, Robert Brout, Peter Higgs, Gerald Guralnik, Carl Hagen and Tom Kibble developed related mechanisms for massive gauge fields. Later particle discovery tested the broader framework; the 1964 papers were theoretical proposals.

Date: Multiple papers in 1964, distinct from the 2012 particle observation. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [CERN: theoretical mechanism and particle discovery](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/higgs-boson/how/)

## 1964 CE · John Bell derives inequalities for local hidden-variable models

ID: `bell` · Quantum theory and information · Mathematics and Physics

A mathematical result made assumptions about locality and hidden variables experimentally consequential. A violation constrains that class of models; it does not enable controllable faster-than-light communication.

Date: Theorem publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel scientific background on entanglement tests](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2022/10/popular-physicsprize2022.pdf)

## 1965 CE · Roger Penrose proves a gravitational singularity theorem

ID: `singularity` · Astronomy and cosmology · Mathematics and Physics

Global geometric reasoning showed that trapped surfaces imply geodesic incompleteness under stated conditions. This is a theorem about a classical theory's limits, not a complete physical description of what happens at a singularity.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original singularity-theorem paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.14.57)

## 1965 CE · Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson report cosmic microwave background radiation

ID: `cmb` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

A persistent microwave signal was interpreted alongside cosmological work as relic radiation from an early hot universe. The observation and the accompanying interpretation involved different groups and complementary contributions.

Date: Publication year of the paired observational and interpretive reports. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1978/wilson/facts/)

## 1967 CE · Steven Weinberg proposes an electroweak model for leptons

ID: `electroweak` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A gauge framework combined weak and electromagnetic interactions with symmetry breaking. Related work by Sheldon Glashow and عبد السلام formed part of the development; later renormalization and experiments were separate achievements.

Date: Publication landmark; associated contributions span several years. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel research summary on electroweak unification](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1979/summary/)

## 1967 CE · Jocelyn Bell Burnell detects the first pulsar signals

ID: `pulsars` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

Repeated radio pulses emerged from a systematic survey and careful scrutiny of unusual data. Their neutron-star interpretation followed; this node records detection rather than assigning the entire discovery to the later prize narrative.

Date: Detection in 1967; the discovery paper appeared in 1968. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original report of a rapidly pulsating radio source](https://doi.org/10.1038/217709a0)

## 1971 CE · Gerard 't Hooft establishes renormalizability of broken gauge theories

ID: `standard-renormalization` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A proof made a crucial class of gauge models suitable for controlled quantum calculations. Together with work by Martinus Veltman, it helped turn electroweak unification into a quantitatively testable framework.

Date: Publication year of the decisive papers. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel research summary on electroweak renormalization](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1999/summary/)

## 1971 CE · Kenneth Wilson develops the renormalization-group account of critical phenomena

ID: `renormalization-group` · Heat and statistical physics · Mathematics and Physics

Changes of scale organized the behavior of systems near phase transitions and explained why different materials can share critical behavior. The framework connected statistical mechanics with methods also central to quantum field theory.

Date: Key publications in 1971; the wider program continued afterward. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1982/wilson/facts/)

## 1973 CE · J. Michael Kosterlitz and David Thouless describe a topological transition

ID: `bkt` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Mathematics and Physics

A two-dimensional model exhibited a transition governed by the binding and unbinding of vortices. Earlier work by Вадим Березинский was an essential part of this history, and the result broadened the usual symmetry-breaking picture.

Date: Publication landmark following related work in 1971–1972. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [APS research collection: topological phases](https://physics.aps.org/topological-phases)

## 1973 CE · Asymptotic freedom reshapes the theory of strong interactions

ID: `qcd` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

Independent calculations by David Gross and Frank Wilczek and by H. David Politzer showed how a non-Abelian gauge interaction weakens at short distances. This became a central ingredient of quantum chromodynamics.

Date: Publication year of the independent calculations. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel research summary on asymptotic freedom](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2004/summary/)

## 1974 CE · Stephen Hawking predicts radiation from black holes

ID: `hawking` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

Quantum-field reasoning in curved spacetime implied thermal radiation associated with black holes. The result joined gravitation, quantum theory and thermodynamics, while astrophysical detection of this radiation remains a separate question.

Date: Initial announcement in 1974; detailed treatment appeared in 1975. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Hawking/)

## 1976 CE · Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken complete a computer-assisted proof

ID: `four-color` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

The four-color theorem was established through a finite reduction and extensive computer checking. The result prompted debate about verification and surveyability, motivating later independent and formal approaches.

Date: Proof announcement in 1976; detailed publications followed in 1977. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor: computer-assisted four-color proof](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Appel/)

## 1977 CE · Ronald Rivest, Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman introduce a public-key system

ID: `rsa` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

Number-theoretic operations supplied a practical route to public-key encryption and signatures. Security depends on precise assumptions and implementation choices; it is not a theorem that every conceivable attack must factor the modulus.

Date: Public announcement in 1977; the journal paper appeared in 1978. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original public-key cryptosystem paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/359340.359342)

## 1980 CE · Klaus von Klitzing measures the integer quantum Hall effect

ID: `quantum-hall` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

A two-dimensional electronic system displayed precisely quantized Hall resistance. The result linked material experiments with fundamental constants and later topological explanations.

## 1982 CE · Richard Feynman argues for simulating physics with quantum systems

ID: `feynman-simulation` · Logic and computation · Mathematics and Physics

The difficulty of efficiently representing quantum systems on classical machines motivated a different computational architecture. The argument was a research proposal, not a completed universal practical quantum computer.

Date: Published paper based on a 1981 lecture. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1994 CE · Peter Shor presents a quantum factoring algorithm

## 2024 CE · ATLAS publishes an observation of top-quark entanglement
