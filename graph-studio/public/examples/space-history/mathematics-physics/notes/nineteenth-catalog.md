# Fields, structures, energy and rigorous analysis: event catalog

## 1801 CE · Carl Friedrich Gauß reorganizes number theory

ID: `gauss-number` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

A systematic treatment of congruences, quadratic residues and related structures gave number theory a durable research framework. The work brought together earlier results and new proofs rather than beginning the study of integers.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Gauss/)

## 1801 CE · Thomas Young presents an interference principle

ID: `young-interference` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A wave account connected superposition with the colors of thin films and related optical phenomena. The famous two-slit demonstration is often used to explain the principle, but its later textbook form should not replace the historical sequence of lectures and experiments.

Date: Lecture landmark; the published lecture appeared in 1802 and further demonstrations followed. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Young_Thomas/)

## 1807 CE · Joseph Fourier submits a mathematical theory of heat

ID: `fourier-memoir` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics and Physics

The submitted memoir proposed representing temperature distributions through trigonometric series. Questions about arbitrary functions and convergence generated mathematical debate; publication of the full treatise followed much later.

Date: Submission year, distinct from the 1822 book. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fourier/)

## 1809 CE · Carl Friedrich Gauß publishes methods for orbital estimation

ID: `gauss-orbits` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

Least-squares reasoning connected observational error with the determination of celestial orbits. Adrien-Marie Legendre had published least squares in 1805, so this event records a major treatment rather than an exclusive first invention.

Date: Publication year; priority and unpublished earlier work require separate treatment. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Gauss/) · [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Legendre/)

## 1812 CE · Pierre-Simon Laplace publishes an analytical theory of probability

ID: `laplace-probability` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

Generating functions, limit methods and inference were organized into an extensive mathematical treatment. Applications to astronomy and error estimation helped link probability with quantitative physical science.

Date: First edition. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Laplace/)

## 1818 CE · Augustin-Jean Fresnel develops a diffraction theory

ID: `fresnel` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A mathematical wave theory explained diffraction patterns and produced a counterintuitive prediction of a bright central spot behind a circular obstacle. The subsequent experimental confirmation strengthened wave optics without settling later questions about the nature of light.

Date: Prize memoir submission; examination and experimental discussion continued in 1819. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fresnel/)

## 1820 CE · Hans Christian Ørsted observes a magnetic effect of electric current

ID: `oersted` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A compass needle responded to a nearby current-carrying wire, establishing a concrete connection between electricity and magnetism. The observation opened a new experimental and theoretical field rather than immediately providing a complete electromagnetic theory.

Date: Experimental announcement year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [NASA educational history of electromagnetism](https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/earthmag/oersted.htm)

## 1820 CE · André-Marie Ampère investigates forces between currents

ID: `ampere` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

Rapidly following the magnetic-current observation, experiments and mathematical models related electrical currents to mechanical forces. The work laid foundations for electrodynamics while using concepts different from the later field formulation.

Date: Initial investigations and announcements; major synthesis followed in 1826. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Ampere/)

## 1821 CE · Augustin-Louis Cauchy publishes a course in analysis

ID: `cauchy-analysis` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Limits, continuity and convergence received systematic treatment, strengthening the foundations of calculus. Some arguments still relied on assumptions later authors would distinguish more carefully, especially uniform convergence.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1822 CE · Joseph Fourier publishes the analytical theory of heat

ID: `fourier-book` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics and Physics

The heat equation and trigonometric expansions linked a physical process with powerful mathematical representations. The work stimulated later analysis far beyond thermal problems, including the study of convergence and function spaces.

Date: Book publication, distinct from the 1807 submitted memoir. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fourier/)

## 1824 CE · Niels Henrik Abel proves a limit of radical formulas

ID: `abel-quintic` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

A proof showed that a general polynomial equation of degree five cannot be solved by radicals. This is a limitation of a specified class of formulas, not a claim that quintic equations have no roots or cannot be approximated.

Date: Publication of the initial proof; a fuller account followed. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Abel/)

## 1824 CE · Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot analyzes heat engines

ID: `carnot` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

An ideal reversible cycle made engine performance a theoretical question about temperature and heat transfer. The argument was initially expressed within caloric theory; later thermodynamics retained the structural insight while replacing that physical account.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Carnot_Sadi/)

## 1827 CE · Georg Simon Ohm relates current, voltage and resistance

ID: `ohm` · Optics and electromagnetic fields · Physics

A mathematical treatment of circuits established a relationship between electrical quantities under specified material conditions. The relation is not universal for every device or regime, but became central to circuit theory.

Date: Publication of the major treatise. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Ohm/)

## 1828 CE · Robert Brown reports persistent microscopic motion

ID: `brownian` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

Observation of suspended particles documented an irregular motion that was not restricted to living material. A quantitative molecular explanation and decisive measurement came later, so this node records observation rather than proof of atomic theory.

Date: Publication describing observations made in 1827. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original report of microscopic particle motion (1828)](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786442808674769)

Changing magnetic conditions produced electrical effects, connecting motion, magnetism and current generation. Later field equations gave a compact mathematical formulation of experimental relationships developed here.
