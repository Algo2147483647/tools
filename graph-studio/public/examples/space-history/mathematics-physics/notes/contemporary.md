# Modern structures, experiments and computation

[Overview](../README.md) · [Event catalog and sources](./contemporary-catalog.md) · [Personal names](../NAMES.md)

After the Second World War, physics increasingly joined large experimental collaborations with highly abstract mathematics. Renormalized quantum electrodynamics demonstrated the power of precise calculation and measurement. Non-Abelian gauge theory then supplied a structure for fundamental interactions and, unexpectedly, tools for the geometry of four-dimensional spaces. The link from `yang-mills` to `donaldson` is a concrete example of physical equations changing pure mathematics.

Symmetry could guide theory and also fail an experimental test. The parity question raised by 李政道 and 楊振寧 in 1956 is separate from the experiment led by 吳健雄 and collaborators reported in 1957. Likewise, gauge-symmetry-breaking papers in 1964 are separate from the ATLAS and CMS particle announcement in 2012. Awards and later recognition do not replace the dates or the distribution of contributions recorded in research publications.

Condensed matter created some of the strongest links to topology. Quantum Hall experiments first established strikingly precise transport behavior. Mathematical invariants then helped explain why such quantities could be robust. Fractional states required interactions and a different many-body account. The graph connects these developments without treating every quantum Hall effect as the same discovery. Vortices, topological phase transitions and conventional superconducting pairs also remain distinct mechanisms.

Quantum information combines foundational questions with new operational proposals. John Bell made a class of assumptions testable through inequalities. Successive photon and spin experiments closed increasingly demanding loopholes. Quantum simulation and factoring algorithms asked different questions about what controllable quantum systems could compute. A theoretical efficient algorithm does not demonstrate the existence of a practical machine at the required scale. The 2024 top-quark entanglement result extends entanglement studies to a new setting; it is not interchangeable with a loophole-free Bell experiment.

Proof verification became a research subject in its own right. The 1976 four-color result used substantial computation, while the later formal proof made logical checking explicit inside a proof assistant. Flyspeck extended this approach to a major sphere-packing result. These are different milestones from discovering a theorem or announcing an informal proof. Similar care separates the repaired 1995 modularity proof from the 1993 announcement, and the 2002–2003 geometric-flow preprints from subsequent community verification.

Modern geometry continues to interact with physical ideas. Quantum field theory gave a new interpretation of knot invariants, and mirror symmetry supplied enumerative predictions later explored rigorously. The eight- and twenty-four-dimensional sphere-packing proofs used harmonic analysis and modular forms in exceptional dimensions. They do not imply a solution in arbitrary dimension. The 2023 monotile result also has a precise scope: the initial construction permitted reflected copies, while a later construction removed that requirement.

The observational route ends with new channels and standards. A gravitational-wave signal detected in September 2015 was announced and published in February 2016; a 2017 neutron-star merger was studied through both waves and light. The 2019 black-hole image was reconstructed from interferometric measurements. In the same year, revised unit definitions fixed fundamental constants while leaving experimental realization and uncertainty analysis essential. These events link theoretical structures, numerical methods, instruments and collective measurement rather than suggesting that one famous equation alone produces a discovery.
