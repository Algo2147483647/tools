# Relativity, quantum theory and mathematical foundations: event catalog

[Period guide](./foundations.md) · [Overview](../README.md) · [Names](../NAMES.md)

## 1900 CE · David Hilbert proposes a research agenda for mathematics

ID: `hilbert-problems` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

A list of problems connected algebra, analysis, geometry and foundations. The sixth problem explicitly asked for axiomatization of physical theories, showing that the mathematical formulation of physics was itself an open research program.

Date: Congress lecture and published problem list; not all 23 problems were spoken in the lecture. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Hilbert/)

## 1900 CE · Max Planck introduces energy elements in black-body theory

ID: `planck` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A derivation of the radiation law used discrete energy elements in a statistical argument. The meaning of quantization evolved through later work; this step was not already the full quantum mechanics of the 1920s.

Date: December presentation landmark. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Planck/)

## 1901 CE · Bertrand Russell identifies a paradox in unrestricted set formation

ID: `russell` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

The contradiction exposed a problem with forming a set from any descriptive condition. It helped motivate type theories and axiomatic set theories, rather than showing that all mathematics was inconsistent.

Date: Discovery year; communication and publication followed. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Russell/)

## 1902 CE · Henri Lebesgue develops measure and a new integral

ID: `lebesgue` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

A new integration theory enlarged the class of functions that could be handled systematically and improved the treatment of limits. It later became fundamental to probability, functional analysis and mathematical physics.

Date: Doctoral thesis landmark. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Lebesgue/)

## 1902 CE · Josiah Willard Gibbs publishes equilibrium statistical mechanics

ID: `gibbs-statistical` · Heat and statistical physics · Mathematics and Physics

Ensemble methods organized the connection between microscopic descriptions and thermodynamic quantities. The framework made probability distributions over states central to the theory rather than treating one mechanical trajectory as sufficient.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Gibbs/)

## 1903 CE · Ivar Fredholm develops a theory of integral equations

ID: `fredholm` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

A general treatment connected solvability with properties of integral operators. Together with related research, it helped create the functional-analytic tools later used for waves, spectra and quantum theory.

Date: Publication of the major paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fredholm/)

## 1905 CE · Albert Einstein proposes light quanta

ID: `einstein-light` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A heuristic argument treated light energy as localized quanta and applied it to the photoelectric effect. The proposal went beyond quantized exchange in a material oscillator and remained controversial before later evidence accumulated.

Date: Publication year; a proposal distinct from later experimental confirmation. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Einstein/)

## 1905 CE · Albert Einstein derives a statistical theory of Brownian motion

ID: `einstein-brownian` · Heat and statistical physics · Mathematics and Physics

A quantitative relation connected diffusion and microscopic fluctuations with molecular parameters. It made suspended-particle measurements a route to testing the reality and scale of atoms.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original 1905 paper on Brownian motion](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19053220806)

## 1905 CE · Albert Einstein formulates special relativity

ID: `einstein-relativity` · Mechanics and gravitation · Physics

A new account of space and time reconciled inertial-frame equivalence with the invariant speed of light. It changed simultaneity and kinematics without including the later theory of gravitation.

Date: Publication year of the electrodynamics paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [AIP historical exhibition on relativity](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/einstein/index_text.htm)

## 1905 CE · Albert Einstein relates mass and energy

ID: `einstein-mass-energy` · Mechanics and gravitation · Physics

A short argument connected a body's energy change with its inertia. The relationship was developed and generalized through later work; it should not be reduced to a claim that all mass was immediately understood in modern field-theoretic terms.

Date: Publication of the separate mass-energy note. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [AIP historical exhibition on relativity](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/einstein/index_text.htm)

## 1906 CE · Андрей Марков studies dependent random sequences

ID: `markov` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

Chains with specified transition probabilities extended probability beyond independent trials. The framework later supported statistical physics, stochastic processes and computation, with applications requiring separate modeling assumptions.

Date: Early publication landmark for the chain method. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Markov/)

## 1908 CE · Hermann Minkowski presents a spacetime geometry

ID: `minkowski` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics and Physics

Special-relativistic relations were organized into a four-dimensional geometric structure. This viewpoint clarified invariant quantities and helped prepare the mathematical language of later relativistic field theories.

Date: Lecture year; the printed account appeared in 1909. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Minkowski/)

## 1908 CE · Ernst Zermelo proposes axioms for set theory

ID: `zermelo` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

Explicit restrictions on set formation sought to preserve useful mathematics while avoiding known paradoxes. The system was an important predecessor of later axiomatizations, not already the final modern formulation.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Zermelo/)

## 1908 CE · Jean Perrin tests molecular theory through particle fluctuations

ID: `perrin` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

Measurements of suspended particles supplied quantitative evidence consistent with molecular statistics. Multiple routes to molecular-scale constants strengthened the argument beyond one visual observation of irregular motion.

Date: Main experimental landmark; reports and synthesis continued afterward. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1926/perrin/facts/)

## 1911 CE · Ernest Rutherford proposes a concentrated atomic nucleus

ID: `rutherford-nucleus` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

Large-angle scattering was explained by concentrating positive charge and most atomic mass in a small region. The model followed earlier experiments and did not yet solve the stability or spectra of atoms.

Date: Model publication; the key scattering report preceded it in 1909. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original 1911 scattering and atomic-structure paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786440508637080)

## 1911 CE · Heike Kamerlingh Onnes observes superconductivity

ID: `superconductivity` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

The resistance of mercury fell abruptly at very low temperature. This established a new material phenomenon whose microscopic explanation required much later quantum and many-body theory.

Date: Experimental discovery year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [AIP discovery history: superconductivity](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/mod/superconductivity/superconductivity.pdf)

## 1913 CE · Niels Bohr proposes quantized atomic states

ID: `bohr` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A model combined a nuclear atom with restricted states and radiation frequencies tied to energy differences. It explained important hydrogen regularities but did not supply the general quantum mechanics later needed for more complex systems.

Date: Publication of the atomic-model papers. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Bohr_Niels/)

## 1913 CE · ஸ்ரீனிவாச ராமானுஜன் sends results to G. H. Hardy

ID: `ramanujan` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

Correspondence initiated a collaboration that brought remarkable work on series, number theory and partitions to a wider mathematical audience. The event marks contact and recognition, not the beginning of the earlier independent investigations.

Date: Dated correspondence landmark. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Ramanujan/)

## 1915 CE · Albert Einstein presents gravitational field equations

ID: `general-relativity` · Mechanics and gravitation · Mathematics and Physics

The metric structure of spacetime became dynamical and linked to energy and momentum. The theory drew on differential geometry and solved important problems while opening new mathematical and observational questions.

Date: November field-equation papers; earlier formulations differed. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [AIP historical exhibition on relativity](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/einstein/index_text.htm)

## 1916 CE · Karl Schwarzschild finds an exact gravitational solution

ID: `schwarzschild` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

A solution for a spherically symmetric vacuum field gave a concrete test of the new equations. The modern physical interpretation of its horizon developed later and should not be assigned fully to the original paper.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Schwarzschild/)

## 1918 CE · Emmy Noether connects continuous symmetries with conservation laws

ID: `noether-symmetry` · Mechanics and gravitation · Mathematics and Physics

A variational theorem related invariance properties to conserved quantities and clarified issues raised by gravitational theories. Its scope depends on mathematical assumptions; it is more precise than the slogan that every visible symmetry yields a new conservation law.

Date: Publication year of the invariance paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [IAS archive: symmetry and conservation](https://www.ias.edu/news/2023/honoring-legacy-emmy-noether)

## 1919 CE · Eclipse expeditions test gravitational light deflection

ID: `eclipse` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

Measurements of star positions near the eclipsed Sun were reported as supporting relativistic light bending. Instrumental uncertainty and data selection matter; later observations provided much stronger tests.

Date: Expedition and announcement year; not the sole or final confirmation of gravitation theory. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original eclipse-expedition report](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009)

## 1921 CE · Emmy Noether reorganizes ideal theory

ID: `noether-rings` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

A structural treatment emphasized chain conditions and general properties of rings and ideals. This helped make abstract algebra a study of relations and transformations rather than only explicit formulas.

Date: Publication of the foundational ideal-theory paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Noether_Emmy/)

## 1922 CE · Stefan Banach develops complete normed spaces

ID: `banach` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

An abstract treatment unified problems involving functions and operators. Such spaces made it possible to transfer arguments between different analytical settings and supplied tools for modern differential equations and physics.

Date: Publication of the dissertation work. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Banach/)

## 1922 CE · Александр Фридман derives evolving cosmological solutions

ID: `friedmann` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

The gravitational equations admitted universes whose spatial scale changes with time. A mathematical possibility preceded the later consolidation of observational evidence for cosmic expansion.

Date: Publication of the first expanding-universe solutions. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Friedmann/)

## 1922 CE · Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach observe discrete beam splitting

ID: `stern-gerlach` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A beam of silver atoms split into distinct components in an inhomogeneous magnetic field. Its modern explanation involves electron spin, a concept not yet available to the original investigators.

Date: Experimental publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1943/stern/facts/)

## 1923 CE · Arthur H. Compton explains shifted scattered X-rays

ID: `compton` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

The wavelength shift could be described through energy and momentum transfer between radiation quanta and electrons. The result strengthened the particle-like account of radiation without eliminating wave phenomena.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original 1923 scattering paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.21.483)

## 1924 CE · সত্যেন্দ্রনাথ বসু develops a new counting method for radiation

ID: `bose` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A statistical argument counted indistinguishable light quanta in a way that reproduced the radiation law. Albert Einstein extended the method to material particles, opening a route to quantum statistics and condensation predictions.

Date: Paper and translation year; the material-gas extension followed in 1924–1925. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Bose/)

## 1924 CE · Louis de Broglie proposes waves associated with matter

ID: `de-broglie` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A doctoral thesis related particle momentum to wavelength and connected quantum conditions with wave behavior. Later diffraction experiments tested this hypothesis; the thesis alone was not experimental verification.

Date: Doctoral thesis year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Broglie/)

## 1925 CE · Wolfgang Pauli formulates the exclusion principle

ID: `pauli` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A restriction on allowed electron states organized atomic structure and spectra. Its later expression in quantum-field theory and its role in matter stability extended beyond the original spectroscopic argument.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Pauli/)

## 1925 CE · Werner Heisenberg, Max Born and Pascual Jordan develop matrix mechanics

ID: `matrix-mechanics` · Quantum theory and information · Mathematics and Physics

A formulation based on observable transition quantities became a noncommutative algebra of physical variables. Recognizing the role of matrices connected a new physical theory to an existing mathematical language.

Date: Sequence of papers during 1925, not a single jointly written founding paper. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Heisenberg/) · [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Born/)

## 1926 CE · Erwin Schrödinger develops wave mechanics

ID: `schrodinger` · Quantum theory and information · Mathematics and Physics

An eigenvalue equation generated quantized energies from wave-like mathematical states. The framework proved related to matrix mechanics, while the physical interpretation of the wave function required further arguments.

Date: Series of papers in 1926. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Schrodinger/)

## 1926 CE · Max Born proposes a probabilistic interpretation of scattering

ID: `born-rule` · Quantum theory and information · Mathematics and Physics

The wave function was related to probabilities of outcomes rather than simply a material wave density. This shifted the role of probability within the theory itself, beyond incomplete knowledge of a classical trajectory.

Date: Scattering papers and their correction established the probability-amplitude interpretation. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Born/)

## 1926 CE · Enrico Fermi and Paul Dirac develop exclusion-based statistics

ID: `fermi-dirac` · Heat and statistical physics · Physics

Independent treatments supplied the statistical distribution for particles obeying the exclusion principle. The framework became essential for electrons in solids, dense stars and many other quantum systems.

Date: Independent publications. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fermi/) · [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Dirac/)

## 1927 CE · Werner Heisenberg states an uncertainty relation

ID: `uncertainty` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

The analysis connected limits on jointly specifying conjugate quantities with the new quantum formalism. Later precise inequalities and debates over measurement distinguish mathematical spread from simplistic claims about defective instruments.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Heisenberg/)

## 1927 CE · Electron-diffraction experiments test matter waves

ID: `matter-diffraction` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

Independent crystal and thin-film experiments found diffraction behavior for electrons. They provided experimental support for the wavelength-momentum relation without turning electrons into ordinary classical waves.

Date: Experimental reports by separate groups. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel research summary on electron diffraction](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1937/summary/)

## 1927 CE · Georges Lemaître connects cosmic expansion with observations

ID: `lemaitre` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

An evolving cosmological model was related to galaxy distances and velocities. The work combined theoretical and observational reasoning before a widely recognized empirical distance-velocity report appeared in 1929.

Date: Publication year; later translations affected its reception. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Lemaitre/)

## 1928 CE · Paul Dirac formulates a relativistic electron equation

ID: `dirac-electron` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A wave equation compatible with special relativity naturally incorporated electron spin and led to new interpretive problems about negative-energy solutions. The later prediction and observation of antimatter were additional steps.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Dirac/)

## 1928 CE · சந்திரசேகர வெங்கட ராமன் and collaborators observe shifted light scattering

ID: `raman` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A small change in the frequency of scattered light revealed exchanges with molecular excitations. The effect opened a powerful spectroscopic method and connected quantum theory with the internal structure of materials.

Date: Experimental discovery and reports. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1930/raman/facts/)

## 1929 CE · Edwin Hubble reports a galaxy distance-velocity relation

ID: `hub-related` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

An empirical relation supported the emerging picture of an expanding universe. Distances and calibrations were later revised, and the observations belonged to a broader history that included earlier spectra and theoretical models.

Date: Publication year, distinct from prior theoretical work on expansion. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original galaxy distance-velocity paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.15.3.168)

## 1931 CE · Kurt Gödel proves incompleteness theorems

ID: `godel` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

Sufficiently expressive, effectively axiomatized consistent systems cannot prove every arithmetic truth, and under suitable assumptions cannot prove their own consistency. These are precise limitations on formal systems, not a general claim that reasoning or mathematical truth is futile.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Godel/)

## 1932 CE · Neumann János organizes quantum theory in Hilbert space

ID: `von-neumann-quantum` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics and Physics

A mathematical synthesis treated states and observables through operators and Hilbert spaces. It clarified the shared structure of quantum formulations while leaving interpretive questions and later technical refinements open.

Date: Publication of the mathematical-foundations book. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Von_Neumann/)

## 1932 CE · James Chadwick identifies the neutron

ID: `neutron` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

Experiments supported an electrically neutral constituent with mass comparable to a proton. The discovery transformed nuclear models and supplied a crucial tool for later studies of nuclear reactions.

Date: Experimental discovery year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1932 CE · Carl D. Anderson observes the positron

ID: `positron` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

Cloud-chamber tracks supported a positively charged particle with electron-like mass. The observation gave a concrete counterpart to the emerging theoretical account of antimatter.

Date: Observation and first publication; a fuller paper followed in 1933. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 1933 CE · Андрей Колмогоров axiomatizes probability using measure theory

ID: `kolmogorov` · Probability and statistics · Mathematics

A systematic foundation represented events and probabilities within a measure-theoretic structure. This unified much existing practice while not determining which probability model describes a particular physical system.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Kolmogorov/)

## 1935 CE · Albert Einstein, Борис Подольский and Nathan Rosen question completeness

ID: `epr` · Quantum theory and information · Physics

A joint paper used correlations to argue that the quantum description was incomplete under its assumptions. Later formulations and experiments turned related issues into sharper tests; the original paper did not present a practical faster-than-light signaling method.

Date: Publication year. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Original 1935 completeness paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.47.777)

## 1935 CE · 湯川秀樹 proposes a particle-mediated nuclear force

ID: `yukawa` · Particles, nuclei and materials · Physics

A theoretical model related the short range of nuclear forces to a massive exchange particle. The later identification of the pion must be separated from the initial proposal and from the intervening discovery of the muon.

Date: Proposal year; pion evidence came in 1947. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [Nobel biographical research summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1949/yukawa/facts/)

## 1936 CE · Alonzo Church and Alan Turing formalize effective computation

ID: `church-turing` · Logic and computation · Mathematics

Independent formal approaches established limits on decision procedures and helped articulate computability. The equivalence of formal models is mathematical; the claim that they capture all effective human procedures has a different logical status.

Date: Publication year of foundational papers; the thesis is distinguished from a theorem. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Church/) · [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Turing/)

## 1939 CE · Lise Meitner and Otto Robert Frisch explain nuclear fission

A physical interpretation connected the chemical products to a dividing nucleus and a large energy release. Naming both interpretation and experiment makes the distribution of intellectual contributions visible.

The first nuclear test and the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki exposed the destructive consequences of organized nuclear research. The history includes civilian suffering, state secrecy and later debates over scientists' political responsibility.
