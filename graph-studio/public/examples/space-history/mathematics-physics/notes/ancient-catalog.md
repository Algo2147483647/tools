## 1850 BCE · The Moscow mathematical papyrus

ID: `moscow-papyrus` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

Problems include the volume of a truncated square pyramid. Practical calculation, training and geometry were already intertwined, although a modern deductive proof cannot simply be read into the surviving procedure.

Date: Approximate manuscript date, not a precise year of discovery. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor historical topic](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Egyptian_papyri/)

## 1800 BCE · Old Babylonian numerical problem solving

ID: `old-babylonian` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

Surviving tablets record place-value sexagesimal calculation, reciprocals and procedures for problems now expressed as quadratic equations. This is evidence of scribal practices, not of modern symbolic algebra or a single inventor.

Date: Approximate period represented by surviving tablets; this point does not date the invention of mathematics. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor historical topic](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Babylonian_mathematics/)

## 1800 BCE · Plimpton 322 and right-triangle numbers

ID: `plimpton` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

This Old Babylonian tablet preserves numerical relations associated with right triangles. Its teaching or computational purpose remains debated; calling it a modern trigonometric table would overstate the evidence.

Date: Approximate Old Babylonian date; interpretation is contested. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor historical topic](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Babylonian_Pythagoras/)

## 1650 BCE · The Rhind mathematical papyrus

ID: `rhind-papyrus` · Arithmetic and algebra · Mathematics

The surviving copy presents fraction arithmetic, distribution and measurement problems, drawing on an older textual tradition. Its unit-fraction methods belong to a different numerical notation from modern decimal calculation.

Date: Approximate copying date; some underlying material is older. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 585 BCE · Θαλῆς and early Greek geometrical traditions

ID: `thales` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

Later sources associate Θαλῆς with geometrical results and an eclipse prediction. These retrospective accounts document a tradition of attribution; they do not establish the methods or exact dates of individual proofs.

Date: Traditional eclipse year used as a reading landmark; mathematical attributions are retrospective. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Thales/)

## 450 BCE · Early Pythagorean mathematics and musical ratios

ID: `pythagorean` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

Greek traditions linked whole-number ratios, musical intervals and geometrical reasoning. The familiar right-triangle theorem was known numerically elsewhere earlier; surviving evidence does not justify assigning its first discovery to one person.

Date: Approximate fifth-century BCE setting; authorship and chronology are uncertain. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Pythagoras/)

## 430 BCE · Ἱπποκράτης and the quadrature of lunes

ID: `hippocrates` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

Work on curved figures showed that selected lunes could be related exactly to rectilinear areas. The result was narrower than squaring the circle, but helped make transformation and proof central to Greek geometry.

Date: Approximate working date reconstructed from later testimony. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Hippocrates/)

## 350 BCE · Εὔδοξος develops ratio theory and exhaustion

ID: `eudoxus` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

A theory of proportion accommodated magnitudes without assuming all lengths were commensurable. Exhaustion arguments compared areas and volumes through increasingly fine bounds, anticipating some problems later treated by analysis.

Date: Approximate fourth-century BCE work; later textual transmission preserves the evidence. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Eudoxus/)

## 350 BCE · Ἀριστοτέλης systematizes motion and natural explanation

ID: `aristotle` · Mechanics and gravitation · Physics

His natural philosophy organized questions about change, place and celestial motion. It shaped later debate for centuries, but its categories should not be treated as an early statement of Newtonian mechanics.

Date: Approximate composition period; the works are not a single dated publication. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Aristotle/)

The Elements assembled definitions, postulates and propositions into a systematic deductive presentation. Its influence concerns the organization of proof as well as geometry; the compilation incorporated earlier mathematical work.

Geometrical reasoning supported propositions about levers and equilibrium. These works connect mathematical proof with idealized physical systems, without implying that every proposition was derived directly from experiment.

## 250 BCE · Ἀρχιμήδης bounds the circumference of a circle

ID: `archimedes-circle` · Analysis and differential equations · Mathematics

Inscribed and circumscribed polygons produced rigorous upper and lower bounds for the circle ratio. The method made approximation compatible with proof, rather than identifying a finite decimal value as exact.

Date: Approximate period; ordered here as a separate mathematical achievement. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

## 250 BCE · Ἀρχιμήδης develops a theory of floating bodies

The surviving treatise relates equilibrium to displaced fluid and investigates floating shapes. The famous bath anecdote is not required as evidence for the mathematical content of the work.

## 240 BCE · Ἐρατοσθένης estimates the size of Earth

ID: `eratosthenes` · Astronomy and cosmology · Mathematics and Physics

A geometrical argument combined a difference in solar altitude with an estimated terrestrial distance. The result illustrates how measurement and assumptions about shape can jointly support an inference about an inaccessible scale.

Date: Approximate date; the length of the ancient stadium and numerical accuracy are debated. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Eratosthenes/)

## 200 BCE · Ἀπολλώνιος develops the theory of conic sections

ID: `apollonius` · Geometry and topology · Mathematics

A systematic investigation of ellipses, parabolas and hyperbolas supplied a durable body of geometry. Their later role in orbital mechanics was a new application, not the original purpose of every proposition.

Date: Approximate composition period. The chart locates this event by year, not by an assumed 1 January date.

Sources: [MacTutor biographical study](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Apollonius/)

## 150 BCE · Ἵππαρχος advances quantitative astronomy

ID: `hipparchus` · Astronomy and cosmology · Physics

Work on chords, solar and lunar models and observations increased the mathematical precision of astronomy. Much of the evidence survives through later authors, so reconstructions of particular tables require caution.
