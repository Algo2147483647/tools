# Sources and editorial conventions

This is a selected educational history through **2025-12-31**, compiled from the primary and institutional sources linked below. It is not an exhaustive launch catalog or a live mission-status service. Each node retains its source URLs; the theme notes list dates together with their mission context.

## Date precision

Most nodes retain UTC calendar-day precision as YYYY-MM-DD. They are positioned at the start of that date for layout; this does not claim an event happened at midnight. Apollo 11 has explicit UTC timestamps for zooming into its vehicle branches. UTC conversion matters: Explorer 1 launched February 1 UTC (January 31 in Florida); Mir launched February 19 UTC (February 20 Moscow); Apollo 16 landed April 21 UTC; Chang'e 3 launched December 1 UTC; Hayabusa2's capsule returned December 5 UTC.

The NSSDC chronology is used to resolve UTC launch dates when a NASA narrative uses a local date. The NASA Moon mission table reports Luna 2's September 13 UTC impact; older summaries sometimes use September 14. Dates are manually curated rather than blindly copied from inconsistent aggregate tables.

## Interval meaning

A thick interval is explicitly named: orbital lifetime, crewed flight, cruise to encounter, sample-return phase or commissioning. An orbital lifetime includes dormant flight and is not a claim of continuous operations. A cruise ends at the encounter, even when the spacecraft continues working. No interval is extended to the present and no unverified current operational status is inferred.

## Relationships

- **sequence:** Recorded chronological order only. Does not assert a causal or technical dependency; selected-milestone links may skip intervening events.
- **part-of:** Source contains the target milestone or hosted flight. Interval endpoints describe the named phase, not necessarily a whole mission.
- **dependency:** A dated boundary starts or completes the named Apollo 11 vehicle phase.
- **rendezvous:** Physical spacecraft rendezvous, docking or assembly, with the named event supplying the time.
- **reference:** A specifically labelled historical, programmatic or same-spacecraft relationship; not a causal assertion.

The timeline includes deliberate chronological links between selected milestones. Filtering does not invent replacement edges across hidden events. The Apollo 11 branches use actual vehicle separation and docking to demonstrate paths that physically rejoin.
