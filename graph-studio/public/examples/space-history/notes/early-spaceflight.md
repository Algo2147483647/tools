# Early spaceflight

Satellites, human orbital flight, spacewalks and rendezvous.

[Workspace guide](../README.md) · [Sources and date conventions](../SOURCES.md)

All 32 events and phases in this theme are included in **space-history.json**. Choose **Early spaceflight** in the Type filter to focus on them within the same timeline.

## Sputnik 1

The first artificial Earth satellite opened the orbital era; its orbital lifetime extended beyond its radio transmissions.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1957-10-04 to 1958-01-04.

- **1957-10-04** — Launch: first artificial satellite
- **1958-01-04** — Atmospheric reentry

[Source 1](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/chronology.html) · [Source 2](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/sputorig.html)

## Sputnik 2

Laika flew aboard this early biological satellite. The interval describes the spacecraft in orbit, not the animal's survival.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1957-11-03 to 1958-04-14.

- **1957-11-03** — Launch with Laika
- **1958-04-14** — Atmospheric reentry

[Source 1](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/chronology.html) · [Source 2](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/chap11.html)

## Explorer 1

The first US satellite carried the radiation experiment associated with discovery of the Van Allen belts. Launch was February 1 in UTC, January 31 in Florida.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1958-02-01 to 1970-03-31.

- **1958-02-01** — Launch: first US satellite
- **1970-03-31** — Atmospheric reentry

[Source 1](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/chronology.html) · [Source 2](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/explorer-1/)

## Vostok 6

Valentina Tereshkova became the first woman in space during a flight that overlapped Vostok 5.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1963-06-16 to 1963-06-19.

- **1963-06-16** — Tereshkova launches
- **1963-06-19** — Tereshkova returns

[Source 1](https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/whos_who_level2/tereshkova.html) · [Source 2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910009709/downloads/19910009709.pdf)

## Voskhod 2

Alexei Leonov performed the first spacewalk; Pavel Belyayev commanded the flight.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1965-03-18 to 1965-03-19.

- **1965-03-18** — Launch
- **1965-03-18** — Leonov's first spacewalk
- **1965-03-19** — Crew returns

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/space-station-20th-spacewalking-history/) · [Source 2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910009709/downloads/19910009709.pdf)

## Gemini IV

James McDivitt and Edward White tested multi-day human flight; White performed the first US spacewalk.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1965-06-03 to 1965-06-07.

- **1965-06-03** — Launch
- **1965-06-03** — White's spacewalk
- **1965-06-07** — Atlantic splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/gemini-iv/)

## Gemini VII

Frank Borman and James Lovell tested two-week endurance and served as the rendezvous target for Gemini VI-A.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1965-12-04 to 1965-12-18.

- **1965-12-04** — Launch
- **1965-12-15** — Rendezvous with Gemini VI-A
- **1965-12-18** — Atlantic splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/gemini-vii/)

## Gemini VIII

Neil Armstrong and David Scott achieved the first docking of two spacecraft, then made an emergency return after an attitude-control failure.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1966-03-16 to 1966-03-17.

- **1966-03-16** — Launch
- **1966-03-16** — First docking with Agena
- **1966-03-17** — Emergency Pacific splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/gemini-viii/)

## Additional milestones

- **1958-03-17 — Vanguard 1 launches.** Solar-powered satellite for geodesy and atmospheric studies. [Source](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/chronology.html)
- **1961-04-12 — Yuri Gagarin's orbital flight.** Vostok 1 carried the first human into space and Earth orbit. [Source](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/april-1961-first-human-entered-space/)
- **1961-05-05 — Alan Shepard's suborbital flight.** Mercury-Redstone 3 carried the first American into space. [Source](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/mercury-redstone-3-freedom-7/)
- **1962-02-20 — John Glenn orbits Earth.** Mercury-Atlas 6 completed three orbits with John Glenn aboard. [Source](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/friendship-7/)
