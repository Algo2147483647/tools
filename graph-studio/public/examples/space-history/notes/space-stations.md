# Space stations

Orbital laboratories, visiting crews and international assembly.

## Salyut 1

The first space station hosted Soyuz 11; the crew died during their return to Earth.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1971-04-19 to 1971-10-11.

- **1971-04-19** — Station launch
- **1971-06-07** — Soyuz 11 crew enters station
- **1971-10-11** — Controlled reentry

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/50-years-ago-launch-of-salyut-the-worlds-first-space-station/)

## Skylab

The US orbital workshop hosted three crews for solar, Earth and human-adaptation research; it remained in orbit after the last crew departed.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1973-05-14 to 1979-07-11.

- **1973-05-14** — Station launch
- **1979-07-11** — Atmospheric reentry

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/skylab/)

## Skylab 2

The first crew repaired the damaged workshop and began its scientific program.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1973-05-25 to 1973-06-22.

- **1973-05-25** — Crew launch
- **1973-06-22** — Pacific splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/navy-recovery-ships-for-human-spaceflight-missions/)

## Skylab 3

The second crew extended the station's medical, solar and Earth-observation investigations.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1973-07-28 to 1973-09-25.

- **1973-07-28** — Crew launch
- **1973-09-25** — Pacific splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/navy-recovery-ships-for-human-spaceflight-missions/)

## Skylab 4

The final crew completed an 84-day flight and extensive science observations.

**Interval:** Crewed flight, 1973-11-16 to 1974-02-08.

- **1973-11-16** — Crew launch
- **1974-02-08** — Pacific splashdown

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/navy-recovery-ships-for-human-spaceflight-missions/)

## Mir

A modular station supported long-duration crews and international visits. The core launched February 19 UTC, February 20 in Moscow.

**Interval:** Orbital lifetime, 1986-02-19 to 2001-03-23.

- **1986-02-19** — Core module launch (UTC)
- **2001-03-23** — Controlled reentry

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/35-years-ago-launch-of-mir-space-stations-first-module/) · [Source 2](https://www.nasa.gov/history/20-years-ago-space-station-mir-reenters-earths-atmosphere/)

## STS-71

Atlantis completed the first Shuttle docking with Mir and exchanged station crew members.

**Interval:** Shuttle flight, 1995-06-27 to 1995-07-07.

- **1995-06-27** — Atlantis launches
- **1995-06-29** — Atlantis docks with Mir
- **1995-07-07** — Atlantis lands

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/sts-71/)

## STS-88

Endeavour carried Unity and joined it to the Russian-built Zarya module, assembling the first two ISS elements.

**Interval:** ISS assembly flight, 1998-12-04 to 1998-12-15.

- **1998-12-04** — Unity launches aboard Endeavour
- **1998-12-06** — Unity and Zarya joined
- **1998-12-15** — Endeavour lands

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/sts-88/)

## ISS Expedition 1

William Shepherd, Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei Krikalev began the station's continuous human presence. This interval ends with their departure, not the station's lifetime.

**Interval:** Expedition aboard ISS, 2000-11-02 to 2001-03-19.

- **2000-11-02** — First resident crew arrives
- **2001-03-19** — Expedition 1 departs aboard Discovery

[Source 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/expedition-1/)

## Additional milestones

- **1998-11-20 — Zarya begins ISS assembly.** The first International Space Station module launched from Baikonur. [Source](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/dec-6-1998-international-space-station-assembly-begins/)
- **2021-04-29 — Tianhe core module launches.** The core module began construction of China's modular space station. [Source](https://english.cas.cn/Special_Reports/rd/2021/202210/t20221017_321657.shtml)
