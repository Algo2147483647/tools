# History of AGI Research

This canvas follows the pursuit of general machine intelligence through **233 events**, **368 relationships**, and **13 research strands**, from the intellectual antecedents of 1666 to documented research in October 2026. It is part of **History of Technology**, alongside the separate spaceflight and mathematics-physics canvases. All explanatory content is in English.

The central story is a sequence of interacting research programs. Logic supplies explicit inference; statistics supplies learning under uncertainty; neural networks supply learned representations; reinforcement learning supplies adaptation through consequences; architectures and robotics assemble memory, reasoning and action. Foundation models bring several of these capabilities into shared systems, while evaluation and alignment expose what broad performance still does not establish.

## Open and explore

In Graph Studio, open **History of Technology** and choose **History of AGI Research** in the workspace graph list. The [timeline](./agi-history.json) opens in Timeline mode with a linked [appearance file](./agi-history.style.json). Existing workspace copies may need **Refresh** to discover the added canvas.

The default is a vertical relation-flow view with an adaptive time axis. Dense periods receive more space; distance is **not proportional to elapsed time**. Use **Track**, **Type**, and **Time range** to inspect one strand or period. Filters retain the shared historical coordinates, so pan toward the period you want and zoom in to read its labels. **Locate selected event** recenters an event after selection; **Show all events** restores the full view. Switch to a linear time axis when comparing durations. Double-click an event for its account, significance, limitations, contributors, date convention and sources.

## Read relationships carefully

**Reading order** links provide chronological navigation within a strand. They do not imply influence or causation, and ordering within a year does not invent a month or day. **Dependency** links identify a specific implementation, input, continuation or successor. **Reference** links explicitly describe a historical connection, debate or conceptual comparison. In particular, “compare” does not assert direct intellectual inheritance. Multiple incoming links identify a meeting of methods; a line crossing without a common node is not a historical convergence.

The scope is the principal research lineages and turning points relevant to AGI. It includes important unsuccessful programs and competing interpretations, rather than every AI paper, application or model release. AGI, consciousness, superintelligence and autonomous deployment are distinct concepts. No event is presented as a universally agreed certification that AGI has been achieved.
