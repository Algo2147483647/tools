# Cognitive architectures and general intelligence

This strand keeps whole-system intelligence visible during periods dominated by specialist machine learning. ACT-R and Soar organize memory, rules, learning and action within architectures. NARS emphasizes adaptation with insufficient knowledge and resources. AIXI instead supplies an idealized decision-theoretic construction whose original form is not computable.

The AGI book, conference and OpenCog community organize explicitly general-intelligence research. Neural Turing Machines explore learnable external memory; Gato investigates one set of weights across modalities and tasks. These contributions answer different questions and should not be assigned a single simple ranking.

Compare three meanings of generality: architectural reuse, shared-model task breadth, and efficient acquisition of new skills. Also compare the Reward is Enough hypothesis with cognitive accounts emphasizing compositionality, causal models and developmental learning. The graph preserves their disagreement rather than declaring one universal definition.
