# Probability causality and statistical learning

Probabilistic AI represents uncertainty rather than demanding complete categorical knowledge. Bayesian networks organize conditional dependence; latent-variable methods and hidden Markov models make uncertainty useful in practical sequence tasks. Stochastic approximation, PAC learning and margin methods clarify different aspects of optimization and generalization.

Causal models address a distinct question: how interventions change outcomes. Observing that two variables covary is not sufficient to know what an action will do. This distinction matters when moving from passive prediction to an agent interacting with the world.

Bayesian program learning brings structured priors and compositional explanations into few-shot learning. The central tradeoff is not probability versus intelligence; it is which assumptions make learning efficient and when those assumptions transfer. Follow links into AIXI, active inference, world models and program induction.
