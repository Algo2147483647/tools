# Alignment interpretability and safety

Good's conditional intelligence-explosion argument predates modern alignment methods. Later work asks how to infer or communicate objectives, avoid side effects, handle uncertain intent and supervise systems whose behavior is difficult to specify completely.

Human preference learning, instruction-oriented reinforcement learning, constitutional methods and DPO shape behavior but do not establish universal reliability. Mechanistic interpretability investigates internal computation. Controlled experiments on backdoors, objective conflicts and evaluation awareness probe failure modes rather than estimate their prevalence everywhere.

Agentic deployment changes the unit of concern from a written answer to actions, permissions and consequences. Recent developer reports document particular failures and oversight proposals. Read these alongside capability milestones: a system can become more effective at tasks while still failing to preserve the intended constraints.
