# Sources and evidence conventions

The timeline links primary papers, original reports, author-hosted materials, publisher records and institutional histories. A publisher record identifies a work; an institutional history may retrospectively identify a founding or project-start date. Recent developer announcements are attributed accounts rather than independent replications.

## Dates and historical interpretation

Dates identify the event described, not the retrieval date. Preprint, conference, journal, demonstration and public-release dates are distinguished when they differ. Year-only markers do not create false day precision. In particular: the Dartmouth proposal is 1955 and the project 1956; reverse-mode differentiation predates the 1986 backpropagation paper; AlphaGo publication, its Lee Sedol match and AlphaGo Zero are distinct; GPT-4 announcement precedes its arXiv report; the R1 report follows the model release; and First Proof reporting follows the initial proof submissions.

Source lists on reference edges point to both endpoints. The relationship label is an editorial historical synthesis or comparison, not a claim that every later author explicitly credits every earlier work. Reading-order links have no causal meaning.
