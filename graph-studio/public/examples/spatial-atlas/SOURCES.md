# Sources and transformations

Sky and city data retrieved 2026-10-21; Beijing rail data checked 2026-10-22. This workspace contains reference examples, with no live network requests when opened.

## All-sky constellations

[d3-celestial by Olaf Frohn](https://github.com/ofrohn/d3-celestial): `stars.6.json`, `starnames.json`, `constellations.json`, `constellations.bounds.json`, `constellations.lines.json` under `data/`. Its documentation identifies the underlying star catalogs and J2000 coordinate conventions. The upstream BSD license is included in [CELESTIAL-LICENSE](./CELESTIAL-LICENSE.txt).

Every catalog star is retained. `space` contains `[1, longitudeDegrees, declinationDegrees]`; longitude represents right ascension wrapped to −180°…180°. Types use catalog constellation identifiers where present, otherwise containment in the source constellation boundaries (including wrapped longitudes and polar regions). Serpens' two regions share one Type. Line vertices match star coordinates to four decimal places; three additional unmatched endpoints are retained explicitly without an invented magnitude. The lines are traditional visual associations, not physical connections or constellation borders.

## World cities

Snapshot retrieved 2026-10-22. The globe contains **8,097 cities across 245 countries and territories**. Coverage includes every **3,708 PPLC/PPLA record** (national capitals and first-order administrative seats) in the [GeoNames cities5000 extract](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/), regardless of population, plus populated places of at least 100,000 people and the original 243 Natural Earth reference places. City sections, abandoned places and historical places are excluded from the population-based additions. The [GeoNames documentation](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt) confirms that this extract includes PPLA seats even below its population threshold. Administrative classes follow the source snapshot; these are not a guarantee that every recent administrative change has been independently verified.

The committed `scripts/world-cities-source.json` retains every selected GeoNames ID, the full capital-ID coverage list, coordinates, language alternatives, economic matches and SHA-256 hashes/URLs for input files. The builder asserts that **no source capital is omitted**. Regression checks cover all 50 US state capitals plus Washington, all 47 Japanese prefectural seats, Germany's 16 states, Brazil's 27 federative units, Australia's 8 states/territories and Canada's 13 provincial/territorial capitals plus Ottawa. Small seats such as Montpelier and Iqaluit are retained. Source classifications may include several seats within one region or a seat shared by multiple regions; city counts are not administrative-unit counts.

[GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) city locations, administrative names and country-specific alternate-name tables are used under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Local-language titles use current, non-colloquial alternate names with source preferred-name flags, supplemented by [Natural Earth's 10m name fields](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/) and the reviewed original-globe endonyms in `scripts/spatial-city-names.json`. Region language preferences are explicit for multilingual countries. Chinese source alternates without language tags may be used when written entirely in Han characters. Where sources lack a local-language form, `nameStatus` explicitly identifies the source-name fallback; no translation is invented. Local alternatives remain in `name_native`, and `name_en` retains the reference name. Natural Earth data are [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/).

Every location uses spherical `[6371, longitude, latitude]` (km/degrees), preserving the source coordinates. Type is the source country/territory's continent. Cities represent independent locations: this expanded globe does not invent transportation or proximity edges.

### Economic point size and intensity

Economic data: **European Commission, Joint Research Centre, GHS-UCDB R2024A V1.2**, socioeconomic theme, field **`SC_GDP_SUM_2020`**, 2020 GDP in purchasing-power-parity units. [Dataset and methodology](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/ghs_ucdb_2024.php), [downloaded theme archive](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_UCDB_GLOBE_R2024A/GHS_UCDB_THEME_GLOBE_R2024A/GHS_UCDB_THEME_SOCIOECONOMIC_GLOBE_R2024A/V1-2/GHS_UCDB_THEME_SOCIOECONOMIC_GLOBE_R2024A_V1_2.zip), DOI [10.2905/1a338be6-7eaf-480c-9664-3a8ade88cbcd](https://doi.org/10.2905/1a338be6-7eaf-480c-9664-3a8ade88cbcd). Authors: Mari Rivero, Melchiorri, Florio, Schiavina, Goch, Politis, Uhl, Pesaresi and colleagues (2024). Reuse is authorised with acknowledgment of the source.

This is modeled **total urban-centre economic output**, not municipal GDP, current-year GDP, income or GDP per capita. The source sums gridded GDP within urban-centre boundaries. We project each city coordinate to World Mollweide (EPSG:54009) and require containment in exactly one UCDB 2025 urban-centre polygon, respecting holes. No nearest-neighbour GDP guess or national-value substitution is used. Several cities inside the same continuous urban centre share its estimate; their values must not be summed. Each matched node retains the urban-centre ID/name and source metric. **5,870 cities match GDP; 2,227 remain visible as hollow points** with missing values, not invented zeros.

Point **area** grows with log GDP: `score = clamp((log10(GDP) - 8) / 4, 0, 1)`, `radius = sqrt(2.25 + 40 × score)`, `opacity = 0.35 + 0.65 × score`. Values below 100 million and above 1 trillion saturate the scale. Hollow points use radius 2.1. Capital labels receive priority over other cities when labels overlap. Type filtering never renormalizes this scale. Depth shading remains a separate camera cue. The workbench legend and node tooltip identify the year, scope and missing-data treatment.

Rebuild entirely offline with `python scripts/build-world-cities.py`. To deliberately refresh the dated source snapshot, run `python scripts/fetch-world-cities.py` after clearing only the relevant cached downloads, inspect changed source records, then rebuild. The main Spatial Atlas generator calls the offline city builder.

## Beijing metro / urban rail network

Checked 2026-10-22 against the [Beijing Subway interactive network map](https://map.bjsubway.com/) and its [structured station/line snapshot](https://map.bjsubway.com/subwaymap/beijing.xml?v=20260630). The source supplies line order, station names, colors and schematic positions. The reviewed extraction is committed in `scripts/beijing-metro-source.json`; `python scripts/build-beijing-metro.py` regenerates this canvas offline. Attribution is retained; the operator does not declare an open-data license in this feed.

Coverage is all 28 through-service routes shown in that map: Lines 1/Batong, 2, 3, 4/Daxing, 5–19, S1, Changping, Fangshan, Yanfang, Yizhuang, Capital Airport, Daxing Airport, Xijiao and Yizhuang T1. Counting Line 1 and Batong separately, and Line 4 and Daxing separately, yields 30 named lines. This covers urban rail, including the two trams and airport services; national rail and suburban railway services are outside this map's scope.

The graph contains 423 unique station-name nodes, 515 rail segments and 106 interchange nodes. Its line memberships total 540, with 128 possible line-pair transfers represented by shared station memberships rather than artificial transfer edges. These counts are not interchangeable with official infrastructure totals. Type filtering uses node `lines` and edge `metadata.line`, so a selected line retains every interchange without including another service's parallel edge. Line colors come from the operator's `lc` values.

### Geographic station coordinates

Coordinates are from [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), retrieved through the [OSM API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/API_v0.6) on 2026-10-22. The attributed extracted coordinate table is committed as `scripts/beijing-metro-coordinates.json`. Every row retains the source object URL, ID, version, timestamp, source name and coordinate method. The generated graph also retains each point's source URL and method.

The source objects were located through the [Beijing subway network relation](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/13495290), its route members, [Line 12](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/18444658) and [Line 18](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/20010805). A mapped station node is preferred where route data include one; otherwise a named rail-stop point inside that station is retained. When several source nodes represent one interchange, one existing source point is selected deterministically, with no averaging, rounding, jitter or visual offset. Airport terminal names are normalized from the source's prefixed names. [Zhufangbei](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1431968152) is represented as a station footprint; its polygon centroid is the representative station point, explicitly identified as such.

`space` is exactly `[longitude, latitude]` in **WGS84 / EPSG:4326**, with the source numeric precision retained. The 2D Cartesian chart is a plate carrée longitude/latitude plot: both axes are angular degrees, not metric distances. All stations, including distant suburban termini, use the same affine mapping. No schematic coordinates from the operator map are used for positioning. GCJ-02 and BD-09 coordinates are not mixed into this WGS84 dataset. Public mapped coordinates have not been independently surveyed; preserving decimal precision does not certify physical accuracy or establish separate platform positions.

Line edges connect consecutive passenger stops with straight segments; they are not surveyed track geometry. A shared node represents an interchange. Muxidi, Dazhongsi, Bagou and Rongchang Dongjie are marked as out-of-station transfers. Fare boundaries, walking routes and timings are not modeled. Labels can be suppressed to avoid overlap, while actual point coordinates remain unchanged. The flat-map camera fits the actual point and label extent instead of fitting the empty margins reserved for 3D orbit views.

Two services sharing consecutive stations retain separate bidirectional service edges. Where necessary, their storage orientations are opposite to satisfy the v3 unique-ordered-pair rule; both retain `direction: "both"`. In the full view, an alternating color overlay keeps both services visible; after line filtering the remaining service is solid.

Only Lines 2 and 10 are closed rings. The feed's erroneous `loop=true` on Line 11 is deliberately overridden. Most links are bidirectional and stored once. Capital Airport service is explicitly Beixinqiao ↔ Dongzhimen ↔ Sanyuanqiao, then Sanyuanqiao → Terminal 3 → Terminal 2 → Sanyuanqiao; the three terminal-loop edges retain and display their direction.

- Line 14 Taoranqiao and Gaojiayuan are not passenger stops in the current network; Line 12 Gaojiayuan is included. [The Yizhuang T1 description](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/renwen/rwzyd/qxdw/lsychmqdd/jkqT1x/202309/t20230927_3268022.html) distinguishes its 14 open stops from the deferred Laoguanli stop.

This is a dated offline snapshot. Planned extensions and unopened station-line combinations are excluded; same-day disruptions and temporary entrance restrictions are not modeled. Rechecking the operator map and reviewed exceptions is required before advancing the snapshot date.

## Shanghai rail network

The 2026-10-22 snapshot includes Metro Lines 1–18, Pujiang, Maglev and the operating Airport Link Line: **21 lines, 24 branch paths, 418 station points, 532 station-line memberships, 512 rail segments and 90 shared interchange nodes**. The Line 11 branch to Huaqiao in Kunshan is included. Jinshan Railway, national rail, Songjiang trams and airside people movers are outside this metro map's scope.

Every passenger stop is checked by line against the operator's [current station roster](https://m.shmetro.com/core/shmetro/mdstationinfoback_new.ashx?act=getAllStations). The four inner/outer ring direction menu entries are not stations. Maglev is separately mapped through its two existing stations. Branch ordering and colors come from [AMap's Shanghai subway feed](https://map.amap.com/service/subway?_175=1&srhdata=3100_drw_shanghai.json), checked against the operator roster and OSM routes. AMap's schematic pixel positions and GCJ-02 coordinates are **not** used as output coordinates.

The extracted snapshot is `scripts/shanghai-metro-source.json`; it retains operator station codes, branch sequences, and each selected OSM node's source URL, object ID, version, timestamp and coordinate method. Source discovery uses the [Shanghai Metro network relation](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/6799988), its route members and small local OSM map extracts for stations missing from those relations. A station node is preferred; otherwise one mapped rail-stop point represents the station complex. Every output `space` pair is exactly the original OSM `[longitude, latitude]` in WGS84, with no rounding, averaging, jitter or suburban compression. No station coordinates are inferred from neighboring stops. Attribution: © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL 1.0](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Public mapped points are not survey-certified platform locations.

The graph preserves the Line 4 ring and the branches of Lines 5, 10 and 11. Common branch trunks appear once per line. Shared Line 3/4 intervals retain separate colored service edges, with opposite storage orientations where needed for the v3 unique-ordered-pair rule. All links remain bidirectional. Type selects every station of a line and only that line's edges. Links join consecutive passenger stops; they do not trace surveyed railway alignments.

Same-named separated sites at **国家会展中心** (Lines 2/17) and **浦东南路** (Lines 2/14, the former Line 2 东昌路) retain distinct points with line-qualified labels. They are not collapsed into a fictitious midpoint or treated as an existing internal transfer. The operator's network connects other shared interchange complexes at one existing source point; this does not model individual platforms, fare barriers, transfer walking paths or times. See the [official explanation of the separate exhibition-center sites](https://swt.fujian.gov.cn/xxgk/jgzn/jgcs/dwmyc/gzdt_446/202410/t20241014_6542574.htm) and [the planning authority's explanation of the Pudong South Road renaming](https://ghzyj.sh.gov.cn/gzcy/lxxd/20241104/60149eef6724456eb4dbebc1af1e9eec.html).
