# Dedekind domains and ideal factorization

Number theory · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `nt.ideals`

## Statement

The ring of integers of a number field is a Dedekind domain. Every nonzero proper ideal factors uniquely into nonzero prime ideals.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Dedekind here means Noetherian, integrally closed and dimension one.

## Example

$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ has unique ideal factorization despite nonunique element factorization.

## Scope and common pitfalls

Unique ideal factorization does not imply every ideal is principal.

## Proof idea

Localize at nonzero primes to obtain discrete valuation rings and recover the ideal exponents.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
