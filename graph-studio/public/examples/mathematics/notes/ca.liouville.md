# Liouville and the fundamental theorem of algebra

Complex analysis · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `ca.liouville`

## Statement

Every bounded entire function is constant. Consequently every nonconstant complex polynomial has a complex root.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Boundedness is global on the entire complex plane.

## Example

If a polynomial had no zero, its reciprocal would be entire and bounded.

## Scope and common pitfalls

A bounded holomorphic function on a proper domain need not be constant.

## Proof idea

Let the radius in the first-derivative Cauchy estimate tend to infinity.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
