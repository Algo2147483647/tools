# Compactness and Hausdorff spaces

Topology · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `tp.compact`

## Statement

A space is compact when every open cover has a finite subcover. A continuous image of a compact space is compact; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Hausdorffness is needed for the closedness conclusion.

## Example

Closed bounded subsets of Euclidean space are compact.

## Scope and common pitfalls

Closed bounded subsets of infinite-dimensional normed spaces need not be compact.

## Proof idea

Pull covers back along the map; use Hausdorff neighborhoods and a finite subcover to separate points from compact sets.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
