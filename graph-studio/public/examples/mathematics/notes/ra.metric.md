# Metric limits and completeness

Real analysis · Definition · Core

Stable ID: `ra.metric`

## Statement

A sequence is Cauchy if its tail has arbitrarily small diameter. A metric space is complete when every Cauchy sequence converges within that space.

## Hypotheses and conventions

The metric, not just the topology, specifies Cauchy sequences.

## Example

$\mathbb Q$ is incomplete in the Euclidean metric; $\mathbb R$ is complete.

## Scope and common pitfalls

Completeness does not imply compactness; bounded sequences in infinite-dimensional spaces need not have convergent subsequences.
