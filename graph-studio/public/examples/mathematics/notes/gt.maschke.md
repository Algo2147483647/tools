# Maschke's theorem

Group theory · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `gt.maschke`

Finite-group representations split into irreducibles when the group order is invertible.

## Statement

If G is finite and the characteristic of k does not divide $|G|$, every finite-dimensional k-representation of G is a direct sum of irreducibles.

## Hypotheses and conventions

The group order must be invertible in the field.

## Example

Over the complex numbers, finite-group representations are completely reducible.

## Scope and common pitfalls

In characteristic p for a group of order divisible by p, invariant complements can fail.

## Proof idea

Average any projection onto an invariant subspace over the group to obtain an equivariant projection.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
