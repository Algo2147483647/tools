# Second incompleteness theorem

Set theory and mathematical logic · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `sl.incompleteness-two`

Consistent effective extensions of PA cannot prove their standard consistency sentence.

## Statement

A consistent computably axiomatized extension T of PA cannot prove its standard arithmetical consistency sentence $\operatorname{Con}(T)$.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Use the usual arithmetized proof predicate satisfying the derivability conditions.

## Example

A stronger theory may prove the consistency of a weaker one without contradiction.

## Scope and common pitfalls

The theorem is sensitive to the consistency formalization and is not a blanket ban on all metamathematical consistency proofs.

## Proof idea

Formalize the diagonal argument inside arithmetic and show that a proof of consistency would yield the unprovable diagonal sentence.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
