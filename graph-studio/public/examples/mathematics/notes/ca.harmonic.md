# Harmonic functions and conjugates

Complex analysis · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `ca.harmonic`

Harmonic functions on simply connected plane domains admit global conjugates.

## Statement

Real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic. A real $C^2$ harmonic function on a simply connected plane domain is the real part of a holomorphic function.

## Hypotheses and conventions

The global conjugate conclusion requires the stated topological condition.

## Example

$\log|z|$ is harmonic off zero but has no global single-valued harmonic conjugate on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$.

## Scope and common pitfalls

Harmonicity is a real second-order condition; holomorphy is a complex first-order condition.

## Proof idea

The Cauchy–Riemann equations define a closed differential form whose periods vanish on a simply connected domain.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
