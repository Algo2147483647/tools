# Lebesgue differentiation theorem

Real analysis · Theorem · Core

Stable ID: `ra.differentiation`

Local averages recover locally integrable functions almost everywhere.

## Statement

For $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^n)$, averages of $|f(y)-f(x)|$ over balls centered at $x$ tend to zero as the radius tends to zero, for almost every $x$.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Lebesgue measure and Euclidean balls are used here.

## Example

For indicators, almost every point of a measurable set is a density-one point.

## Scope and common pitfalls

The conclusion is almost everywhere and cannot be extended to arbitrary metrics and measures without hypotheses.

## Proof idea

Approximate locally by continuous functions and control exceptional points with a maximal inequality.

This is a proof strategy; details and intermediate lemmas are in the references.
