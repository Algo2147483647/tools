# Measure spaces

Real analysis · Definition · Core

Stable ID: `ra.measure`

Countably additive measures assign sizes to sets in a sigma-algebra.

## Statement

A measure $\mu:\Sigma\to[0,\infty]$ is countably additive on a sigma-algebra and satisfies $\mu(\varnothing)=0$. A measurable function pulls Borel sets back into $\Sigma$.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Countable additivity applies to pairwise disjoint measurable sets.

## Example

Counting measure, Lebesgue measure and probability measures have different total masses.

## Scope and common pitfalls

A sigma-algebra is closed under countable unions; arbitrary unions need not be measurable.
