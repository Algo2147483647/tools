# Derived series and solvable groups

Group theory · Definition · Core

Stable ID: `gt.solvable`

Repeated commutators eventually vanish in a solvable group.

## Statement

The derived series is $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$. A group is solvable if this reaches the identity after finitely many steps.

## Hypotheses and conventions

Commutator subgroups measure failure of commutativity.

## Example

$S_3$ is solvable; $A_5$ is not.

## Scope and common pitfalls

Solvable does not mean abelian, and finite groups are not all solvable.
