# Laurent series and isolated singularities

Complex analysis · Classification · Core

Stable ID: `ca.singularities`

## Statement

An isolated singularity is removable, a pole, or essential according to whether the Laurent principal part is zero, finite nonzero, or infinite.

## Hypotheses and conventions

The function is holomorphic on a punctured disk.

## Example

$\sin z/z$, $1/z^2$, and $e^{1/z}$ illustrate the three cases at zero.

## Scope and common pitfalls

A branch point such as zero for a logarithm is not an isolated singularity of a single-valued holomorphic function on a punctured disk.
